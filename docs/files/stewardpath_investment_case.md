## 1. The thesis in one line
A demographically certain, time-bound wave of business owners must transition ownership over the next 10–15 years; the overwhelming majority are unprepared; and no one owns the **upstream, trust-first, unregulated preparation layer** that sits before brokers, valuations, and advisors ever enter the picture. StewardPath owns it.

## 2. Why now — the tailwind is structural, not speculative
The "Silver Tsunami" of business-owner retirement is one of the most predictable macro shifts in the economy:
- Roughly **10,000 Americans turn 65 every day**, and an estimated **2.3 million+ U.S. businesses are owned by baby boomers**, employing tens of millions of people.
- Widely cited owner-readiness research (e.g., the Exit Planning Institute's *State of Owner Readiness* studies) finds that **~70–80% of owners have no formal transition or succession plan**, and that **roughly 80% of a typical owner's net worth is locked inside the business**.
- Of the businesses that do go to market, **only a minority actually sell** — many simply close, destroying jobs and value.
- Estimates commonly place **trillions of dollars in business value** in transition over the coming decade as this generation steps back — value that is routinely eroded by unpreparedness.

Demand here is not a question of *whether* but *when*, and the *when* is now arriving in volume. That is exactly the kind of non-discretionary, demographically driven wave investors look for.

## 3. The problem and the gap
Owners approaching a transition are not only asking "how much can I sell for?" They are asking: what happens to my employees, will the buyer damage what I built, will my family understand, what if I wait too long, what should I keep private, what must I prepare *before* I talk to anyone. These are functional, emotional, and social needs at once.

Today's solutions all start at the **transaction**: brokers, valuation calculators, M&A advisors, and exit-planning consultants are expensive, intimidating, and arrive once the owner is already exposed. Nothing serves the anxious, private, **pre-transaction** moment — the point where an owner first realizes they need to get ready but isn't willing to call a broker or hand over their financials. That gap is the wedge.

## 4. Solution and positioning
StewardPath is a private readiness-and-preparation system that helps owners decide what must be protected and get ready *before* pressure defines the terms. Positioning strategies:
- **Protection-first, not transaction-first** — "Before you sell, decide what must be protected." This reframes the category away from price and toward what owners actually fear losing.
- **Upstream of, and partnered with, the transaction ecosystem** — StewardPath does not compete with brokers and advisors; it feeds them prepared, qualified owners. That makes the incumbents a distribution channel, not a threat.
- **Trust and confidentiality as the brand moat** — for a paranoid, high-stakes audience, visible confidentiality is the product, and it is precisely what the transaction-first players neglect.
- **Category creation** — "transition readiness / ownership stewardship," not "exit-planning software."
- **Two-sided brand** — owner-facing experience plus an advisor-facing tool.

## 6. Business model — own the unregulated preparation layer
The strategic core: monetize **preparation**, deliberately *not* the transaction. StewardPath is not a broker, valuation firm, or licensed advisor — it routes all regulated work to humans. This is a feature, not a limitation:
- **No licensing burden** → faster to launch, cheaper to operate, simpler liability, easier to scale across geographies.
- **Software economics** on a problem the incumbents solve with expensive human hours.

Three revenue lines, three buyers, declining dependence on any one:
1. **Owner readiness product** *(core, unregulated)* — self-serve report (~$249–499) and AI-delivered concierge package (~$1,500–3,000+). Because the concierge experience is delivered by an AI system with a single thin human touchpoint, gross margins run **~85–90%** versus ~30–40% for human exit-planning consultants — and it scales where they cannot.
2. **Advisor subscription** *(distribution + recurring revenue)* — CPAs, planners, and banks use StewardPath with their owner clients (~$199–999/mo). This channel borrows the advisor's existing trust, solving the hardest problem in the business: getting a guarded owner to engage.
3. **Qualified warm-handoff / deal-flow** *(upside)* — a prepared, readiness-scored owner is the most valuable lead in lower-middle-market M&A; introducing them to vetted advisors/brokers is monetizable. *(Structure this carefully against referral-fee regulation; treat as optionality, not the base case.)*

The model deliberately separates "preparing the seller" (unregulated, scalable, ours) from "executing the transaction" (regulated, someone else's).

## 7. Why it's venture-viable — the moat
- **AI-native margins** — concierge-grade outcomes at near-zero marginal cost.
- **Data network effect** — as readiness data accumulates across thousands of owners, StewardPath can benchmark a new owner against peers ("owners like you typically have these gaps"), a defensible advantage no individual consultant can match and a feature that improves with scale.
- **Trust as a moat** — being the brand owners trust with their most sensitive decision is hard to displace and compounds via referrals.
- **Category ownership** — defining "transition readiness" lets StewardPath set the language, the score, and the standard.
- **Multiple expansion vectors** — owner segments → advisor channel → geographies → adjacent moments (the longitudinal program, re-scoring, the prepared-deal-flow marketplace).

## 8. Growth trajectory
Sequenced expansion: prove direct-owner value in the U.S. core band → scale via the advisor channel (recurring, trust-borrowing) → layer the longitudinal program and re-scoring (higher lifetime value) → activate benchmarking and the prepared-deal-flow layer (the moat and the upside) → expand into English-speaking markets facing the identical demographic wave. Each stage compounds the last.

## 9. Capital efficiency and validation discipline
This is not a "build it and hope" plan. The go-to-market is staged to de-risk the riskiest assumptions cheaply, before heavy spend:
- **Phase 0 (no build):** concierge-by-hand validation with 5–10 design-partner owners to confirm they will share sensitive data, find the output valuable, and **pay real money** — using actual commitments (deposits, pre-orders), not verbal interest.
- **Phase 1 MVP:** the smallest self-serve product that proves completion, value, and payment.
- **Phases 2–3:** the longitudinal program, advisor channel, and the benchmarking moat.

Investors and grant committees should read this as evidence of disciplined, hypothesis-driven execution and low burn-to-signal.

## 10. Risks and mitigations
- **Trust / adoption risk** (will guarded owners engage?) → mitigated by confidentiality-first design and, decisively, the advisor channel that borrows existing trust.
- **Willingness-to-pay risk** → tested directly in Phase 0 with real commitments before scaling.
- **Founder-dependency / single-channel risk** → three revenue lines and two buyer types.
- **Regulatory line** → stay strictly in the unregulated preparation layer; route regulated work to licensed humans.
- **Incumbent response** → incumbents are positioned as partners/distribution; the upstream, emotional, pre-transaction moment is not where they play.

## 11. How to angle the pitch by funder
Different funders reward different things; lead with the frame that matches the room.

- **Venture capital** — emphasize venture-scale outcome: the demographic TAM, AI-driven margins, the data network-effect moat, category creation, and the geographic/expansion path. The story is "a structurally growing market that incumbents serve with expensive human labor, captured by software with a compounding data advantage."
- **Angel investors** — emphasize the founder, the capital-efficient validation plan, the sharp wedge, and the speed to a first paying cohort. Angels back conviction and momentum; show the Phase 0 commitments.
- **Incubators / accelerators** — emphasize team, learning velocity, and the rigor of the hypothesis-driven rollout. The disciplined Phase 0 design is itself the selling point — it signals founders who test before they build.
- **Government grants (economic development / small-business / rural)** — reframe around **public economic impact**: when owners can't prepare and can't sell, businesses *close*, and **jobs and community anchors disappear** — disproportionately in rural and main-street economies. StewardPath helps preserve viable businesses and the employment they sustain through the largest ownership-transition wave in modern history. Quantify jobs-at-risk in the target region.
- **Nonprofit / philanthropic foundations** — emphasize **mission**: protecting employees, families, and local economies through a vulnerable transition; serving owners who can't afford expensive advisors (an equity gap); and — a strong, well-funded hook — surfacing **employee ownership** as a viable succession path. Foundations and policy funders actively support employee-ownership transitions as a tool for job preservation and broad-based wealth; StewardPath's neutral readiness layer naturally elevates that option for owners who'd otherwise never consider it.
