# StewardPath — Phase 0 Validation Test Plan (Concierge-by-Hand)

## Purpose
Before building Phase 1, find out — cheaply and honestly — whether the business is real. Phase 0 is **Wizard-of-Oz**: you deliver the StewardPath experience *by hand* to 5–10 design-partner owners (no product built), and watch what they actually do. The goal is to **try to disprove** the idea, not to collect encouragement.

## The four questions Phase 0 must answer
This whole phase exists to answer four things, in priority order (riskiest first):

1. **Will they share?** Do owners actually hand over sensitive financial, family, and succession information to a system they don't know? (If not, nothing downstream matters.)
2. **Is the output valuable?** Once they see the readiness score, rationale, and advisor-ready summary, does it change what they do next — or just make them nod politely?
3. **Will they pay?** Not "would you pay" — do they actually put money or a hard commitment down?
4. **At what number?** Where does real willingness to pay sit, and which tier (self-serve vs. concierge) do they self-select into?

---

## Hypotheses, signals, and go/kill thresholds
State each as something that can fail. Set the threshold *before* you run, so you can't rationalize a weak result afterward.

| # | Hypothesis (falsifiable) | Real signal to watch (not opinions) | Suggested go threshold* |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 Share | Owners will disclose financials, family dynamics, and succession intent in a guided session | % who complete the sensitive sections without bailing; do they give real numbers/specifics or vague deflections | ≥ 60% of sessions reach a complete, specific picture |
| H2 Value | The output changes the owner's next action | Do they take a concrete next step within ~2 weeks (book an advisor call, fix a named gap, share the summary)? Do they ask to keep it? | ≥ 50% take a concrete next action; most ask to keep the output |
| H3 Pay | Owners will pay real money for this | Actual pre-payment, deposit, or signed commitment — NOT verbal "yes" | ≥ 2–3 of 5–10 convert to a real commitment |
| H4 Price | There's a defensible price and a clear tier split | The number they commit to; whether they pick self-serve vs. concierge | A repeatable number emerges, not noise |

*Thresholds are starting guesses for a 5–10 person test — adjust to your risk appetite, but write them down first.

---

## Who to recruit (and who to avoid)
- **Target:** founder-led owners, ~10–100 employees, who are *actively thinking* about a transition in the next 1–3 years. Mix of family businesses and non-family.
- **Best sources:** warm intros from CPAs, exit planners, community bankers, and industry/trade associations — these people already sit on the trust the product needs, and they're your eventual channel.
- **Avoid:** friends, anyone who'll be nice to you, owners with zero transition intent (they'll give polite, useless feedback), and people who only want free advice.
- **Number:** 5–10 is right for Phase 0 — enough to see a pattern, few enough to run by hand. You're looking for *consistency of signal*, not statistical significance.

---

## The session protocol (run it like the real product would feel)
Keep it ~60–75 minutes, recorded with permission. You are the "system."

1. **Trust opening (5 min).** Deliver the pre-intake reassurance verbally exactly as the product would: what you'll ask, that it stays private, that they control all sharing, nothing goes to employees. *Watch whether this visibly lowers their guard — that reaction is itself H1 data.*
2. **Guided intake (30–40 min).** Walk the branching intake by hand. Use the reflective-summary moves out loud ("So what I'm hearing is…"). Note **exactly where they hesitate, deflect, or go vague** — those friction points are gold.
3. **Deliver the output (15 min).** Hand them a real readiness score + rationale + a short advisor-ready summary you prepared (you can build this manually after a short break, or pre-draft live). Watch their face and their questions.
4. **The ask (10 min).** Make a real offer with a real price (see below). Stop talking and let them respond.

---

## How to measure each question without fooling yourself
The single biggest risk in Phase 0 is **false positives from politeness.** Guard against it:

- **For H1 (share):** measure *behavior*, not consent. Did they actually give specific financials and name the family tension, or did they smile and stay vague? Vagueness is a soft "no."
- **For H2 (value):** the test is the **two-week follow-up**, not the in-room reaction. "That's helpful" is worthless; "I called my accountant / I started documenting X / can I send this to my attorney?" is real. Ask at the end: *"What will you do differently because of this?"* and then check whether they did.
- **For H3 (pay):** never accept a verbal yes. Use a **real commitment**: a deposit, a pre-pay at a founding-member discount, or a signed letter of intent to buy at launch. Money or a signature is the only honest signal. A "yes I'd pay" with no commitment counts as a **no**.
- **For H4 (price):** don't ask "what would you pay" (people lowball or guess). Instead **present anchored prices and watch the flinch.** Offer two real tiers — e.g., a self-serve report at one number and a concierge package at a higher one — and see which they reach for and where they hesitate. The tier they pick tells you as much as the number.

**Mom-Test discipline throughout:** ask about what they've *already done* (past behavior predicts; opinions don't). "Have you ever paid for help preparing for this?" beats "Would you pay for this?" Avoid leading questions; never pitch and ask for validation in the same breath.

---

## What to instrument / capture per session
- Where in the intake they hesitated or deflected (timestamped notes).
- Whether they gave specifics vs. vagueness on financials, family, succession.
- Their unprompted language describing the problem (verbatim — this feeds your JTBD marketing later).
- Their reaction to the output and the *specific* next action they named.
- The exact offer made, the price, the tier, and the actual commitment (or not).
- Two-week follow-up outcome.

---

## Decision rule (what Phase 0 outputs)
After 5–10 sessions, one of three calls:

- **GO to Phase 1** if H1 and H3 both clear threshold (they share *and* they commit money). Value and price details refine the build, but share + pay is the gate.
- **PIVOT** if they share willingly and value the output (H1, H2 strong) but won't pay at any workable number (H3/H4 fail) — the value is real but the model/price/channel is wrong. Likely move: lean into the advisor channel (someone else pays) before direct-to-owner.
- **STOP / rethink** if they won't share (H1 fails) — the trust premise is broken, and no pricing or feature work fixes that. This is the cheapest, most valuable possible failure to discover now rather than after building.

## Common ways this test lies to you (avoid)
- Talking to people without real transition intent → polite, meaningless enthusiasm.
- Accepting "I'd definitely pay" as validation → only money/commitment counts.
- Leading questions and pitching → you'll hear what you want.
- Counting "that's interesting" as value → require a named next action.
- Running too few of the *right* people, or too many of the wrong ones.

## Timeline
Recruiting + 5–10 sessions + two-week follow-ups ≈ **3–4 weeks**, no engineering. That's the whole point: you learn whether Phase 1 is worth building before you write it.
