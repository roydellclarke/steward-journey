# StewardPath — Beachhead Decision (DE Steps 1–2)

> A hypothesis to validate in Phase 0, not a permanent commitment. Adjust the vertical if you have a domain you know better than home-services — the logic transfers.

## Chosen beachhead
**Founder-led home-services / trade businesses** — lead vertical **HVAC** *(swappable for plumbing, electrical, or a trade you know better)* — roughly **15–75 employees**, owner **55+**, who are **getting or fearing unsolicited acquisition offers** (active PE roll-up territory). **National, online-led.**

## Why this segment (scored against DE's criteria)
- **Ability to pay:** High — strong cash flow, real enterprise value.
- **Reachable + word-of-mouth:** High — tight national trade communities, associations, owner peer groups, suppliers, and trade media; owners reference each other.
- **Compelling reason to buy *now*:** Very high — home-services is a hot PE roll-up sector, so the sharpest trigger (the unsolicited offer) is firing across the whole segment, and owners feel unprepared and outmatched.
- **Product fit:** High — these are exactly the founder-dependent, undocumented businesses your readiness product is built for.
- **Leverage to adjacent markets:** High — the message and product transfer almost unchanged to adjacent trades.

## Narrowing discipline (critical for a national play)
Because you're national (not local), homogeneity must come from **vertical + situation, not geography.** Go after ONE trade + the unsolicited-offer trigger — "HVAC owners in the roll-up crosshairs" — *not* "all trades everywhere." Dominate that, then expand to adjacent trades.

**Honest caution:** national/online-led is the harder beachhead shape. Beachheads compound on dense word-of-mouth inside a tight group, and geography usually supplies that tightness. National removes the local glue, so your tightness must come entirely from the narrow vertical + trigger, and your word-of-mouth must travel through national channels. National + broad segment = no compounding.

## Acquisition channels (national, online-led)
- **Primary top-of-funnel:** the trigger→question→content/AI-search engine (from the discoverability playbook). Own the answer to *"I got an offer for my HVAC business — am I ready, and how do I not get taken advantage of?"*
- **National trade associations, owner peer groups, trade media/podcasts.**
- **Advisor channel** (CPAs, bankers, planners serving the vertical) — borrows trust and cuts cost of acquisition.

## Phase 0 implication
Recruit your 5–10 design-partner conversations from **this segment** — founder-led HVAC (or chosen trade) owners 55+, ideally ones who've received an offer or have a peer who sold. A single, narrow segment makes every interview sharper and the findings directly comparable, instead of averaging noise across wildly different business types.

## Adjust if
You have a vertical you know better than home-services (a trade, industry, or community you understand or can reach): swap it in as the lead vertical. The structure — one vertical + the offer trigger + national community + content/advisor channels — stays the same.
