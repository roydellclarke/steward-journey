# StewardPath — Trigger Map & AI-Era Discoverability Playbook

> **Sequencing caveat:** the trigger list below is a *hypothesis* to validate, not gospel. Your Phase 0 and past-seller interviews are where you confirm which triggers actually dominate ("what finally made you act?"). Capture this map now — it sharpens JTBD, marketing, and product — but treat the content/marketing build as Phase 1–2, after Phase 0 confirms the language. The durable advantage is genuinely useful, authoritative content; standards and crawler names churn, good answers don't.

## 1. Trigger → Question → Loss-aversion angle
The unifying principle: owners act from fear of loss far more than hope of gain. Each trigger produces a question in the owner's own words — that question is both your marketing angle and a content asset to own in search and AI answers.

| Trigger | The question they ask (their words) | Loss-aversion angle to lead with |
|---|---|---|
| **Unsolicited offer / broker approach** *(sharpest)* | "Someone offered to buy my business — is it a good deal? Am I ready? How do I not get taken advantage of?" | Don't get lowballed by a buyer who knows far more than you; don't get rushed into an irreversible mistake. |
| **Peer just sold** | "How do I find out what my business is worth and whether I'm ready?" | Don't leave money — or your people — on the table the way you've heard others did. |
| **Health scare (self/spouse)** | "What happens to my business if something happens to me? How do I get ready in case I have to step back?" | Don't let everything you built unravel if you're suddenly not there. |
| **Peer's death/illness** | "I'm not getting younger — what should I be doing now?" | Don't wait until it's an emergency and your options have shrunk. |
| **Burnout / lost passion** | "I'm tired of running this — what are my options to step back?" | Don't let exhaustion push you into a fire-sale on someone else's terms. |
| **Key person leaving** | "The business depends on me/them — can I even sell it like this?" | Don't discover your dependence on one person *after* a buyer does. |
| **Kids don't want it** | "What do I do with the business if my family won't take it over?" | Don't let the place close — and the jobs vanish — for lack of a plan. |
| **Wealth locked up** | "Most of my money is tied up in the business — how do I get it out safely?" | Don't keep your life savings exposed to a single illiquid asset. |
| **Industry consolidation** | "Competitors are getting bought — should I sell now or wait?" | Don't get left small and stranded while the market moves past you. |
| **Tax / timing window** | "Should I sell before the rules change?" | Don't miss a window and watch the after-tax outcome shrink. |

## 2. Where they start looking
- **Trusted humans first** (this audience): accountant (the #1 first call for SMB owners), then attorney, financial advisor, banker, and peers who've already sold. → This is why the advisor channel and "going to them" stay essential.
- **Self-directed research, increasingly AI-first:** a question typed into Google (now answered by AI Overviews / AI Mode), or directly into ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or Claude — which return a *synthesized answer*, not a list. YouTube and trade media fill in around it.
- **The shift that matters:** at the moment of highest intent, an engine composes the answer. If StewardPath isn't cited or surfaced in that answer, it doesn't exist for the self-directed researcher.

## 3. AI-era discoverability checklist (GEO)
*(Standards and crawler names move fast — verify current details before implementing; this reflects early-2026 knowledge.)*

**robots.txt — allow the right crawlers.** To appear in AI answers you must *not* block the engines you want to be in. Consciously allow the major AI crawlers (names change — verify): OpenAI's GPTBot / OAI-SearchBot, Anthropic's ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Microsoft/Bing (Copilot), Applebot-Extended, and note Google's nuance — normal Googlebot indexing powers AI Overviews, while **Google-Extended** governs Gemini's use of your content. Many sites accidentally block these; decide deliberately.

**llms.txt + markdown mirrors.** Publish an `llms.txt` at your site root — a clean, curated markdown map of your most important pages/answers so models can find and parse them. Consider `.md` versions of key pages and an `llms-full.txt`. *Honest caveat:* `llms.txt` is an emerging, proposed convention and is not yet universally honored by the big engines — treat it as cheap, low-risk hygiene, not a guaranteed channel.

**Structured, authoritative Q&A content.** This is the part that actually works and survives standard churn. For each trigger-question in Section 1, publish a clear, genuinely useful answer page in the owner's language. Use clean semantic structure (real headings, concise direct answers up top), FAQ/Q&A formatting, and schema.org structured data (FAQPage, Article, Organization). Engines and humans both reward content that answers the question directly and credibly.

**Authority signals.** Original data (e.g., your readiness benchmarks), named expertise, and citations make you more likely to be quoted by an AI engine, not just indexed.

## 4. Loss-aversion copy principles
- Lead with what they stand to *lose*, not what they'll gain.
- Name the specific fear (lowballed, employees mistreated, forced timing, eroding value, legacy mishandled).
- Reassure on confidentiality in the same breath (it's preparation, private, no obligation).
- Keep it calm and non-rushed — pressure tactics repel this audience and contradict the brand.

## 5. The beachhead content to own first
The unsolicited-offer moment — "I got an offer for my business; how do I know if I'm ready and not being taken advantage of?" — is the single richest target: highest intent, highest loss-aversion, and a perfect match for the "prepare before pressure defines the terms" wedge. Own that question across an answer page, the advisor talk-track, and the AI/search layer before broadening to the others.
