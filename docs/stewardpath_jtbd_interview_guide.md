# StewardPath — Phase 0 JTBD Interview Guide

## How to use this (read first)
- **Talk 20%, listen 80%.** Your job is to get them telling stories.
- **Chase specifics, not opinions.** "Tell me about the last time…" beats "Do you usually…". Past behavior predicts; opinions about the future don't.
- **Follow the emotion.** When their voice changes or they pause, slow down: "Say more about that." That's where the real job lives.
- **Capture verbatim.** Write down their exact words — especially how they describe the problem, what they fear, and what they want to protect. That phrasing is your future marketing language.
- **Never pitch.** If they ask what you're building, give one sentence and steer back to them. You are here to learn, not to sell or correct.
- **Don't ask "would you buy this."** Ask what they've already done and paid for.
- **Watch their openness itself** — how freely they answer the financial and emotional questions IS your "will they share?" signal.

You won't ask all of these. Pick up the thread they give you and follow it.

## Opening (put them at ease)
- "There are no right answers here — I just want to understand how you think about this. Mind if I take notes?"
- "Tell me a bit about the business — how it started and what it's become."

## 1. The trigger — when it first became real
- "When did the thought of eventually selling or stepping back first cross your mind? What was going on at the time?"
- "Was there a specific moment or event that made it feel more real?"
- "How often does it cross your mind now, compared to a year ago?"

## 2. The struggle — the push
- "When you think about the business changing hands someday, what's the hardest part to think about?"
- "What feels unresolved or up in the air about it right now?"
- "What worries you most — about waiting too long, or about it happening on someone else's timing?"

## 3. What they've actually done (the gold)
- "What have you done about it so far — even small things?"
- "Have you talked to anyone about it — an advisor, family, a peer? How did that go?"
- "Tell me about the last time you looked into this. What did you do, and what happened?"

## 4. The emotional job
- "When you picture handing it over, what do you feel?"
- "What, if anything, keeps you up at night about it?"
- "Is there a part of this that's hard to talk about, even with people close to you?" *(They needn't answer — you're listening for whether one exists.)*

## 5. The social job — who they're protecting and how they want to be seen
- "When you imagine the transition, who are you thinking about — who do you most want to do right by?"
- "How would you want your employees, customers, and family to see the decision?"
- "What would 'handling it well' look like? And what's the version — like 'selling out' — that you'd never want to be?"

## 6. The functional job — their definition of 'ready'
- "If you woke up tomorrow and the business was truly 'ready' to sell or hand off, what would be true that isn't true today?"
- "What do you think a buyer would want to see that you're not sure you have?"
- "What would you want figured out before you ever sat across from a buyer or broker?"

## 7. Anxieties & what holds them back
- "What's stopped you from doing more about this already?"
- "What would have to be true for you to feel safe even starting to prepare?"
- "What makes you hesitate to bring other people into it?"

## 8. Money & value (ask about the PAST first)
- "Have you ever paid for help with anything like this — a consultant, advisor, planning work? What did it cost, and was it worth it?"
- "What would make spending money to get ready feel obviously worth it to you?"
- "If something could help you prepare and protect what matters — what would make you trust it, and what would make you walk away?"

## 9. The desired outcome — the magic wand
- "If you had a magic wand for this whole thing, what would it do for you?"
- "A year from now, what would make you say, 'I'm glad I started preparing when I did'?"

## Closing
- "What should I have asked you that I didn't?"
- "Who else do you know who's wrestling with this that I should talk to?"

## Avoid (these quietly ruin the data)
- Leading questions ("Don't you think it's hard to…?").
- Treating hypotheticals about the future as evidence.
- Talking about your solution.
- Letting a polite "that's interesting / I'd use that" stand in for a real story or a real past action.
