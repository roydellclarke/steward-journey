# StewardPath — HVAC / Plumbing Language Bank (beachhead)

> A starting hypothesis. The real, winning phrases come verbatim from your Phase 0 and past-seller conversations — capture how owners actually talk and replace these. Use trade language on the owner-facing front end; the polished terms ("readiness program," "succession") are fine for the advisor channel.

## The golden rule
Talk like the shop, not like the buyer. Drop the MBA/PE vocabulary — it signals you're one of *them*. Plain trade talk signals you're one of *us*.

## Say this, not that
| Instead of (buyer/corporate) | Say (trade) |
|---|---|
| exit strategy / liquidity event | selling the shop / getting out / stepping back |
| transition / succession readiness | getting ready to hand it off |
| valuation / enterprise value / EBITDA | what your shop is worth to a buyer |
| employees / workforce / staff | your crew / your guys / your techs |
| customer base / clientele | your customers / your regulars |
| owner-dependency / key-person risk | whether it can run without you / when you're off the truck |
| recurring revenue | your maintenance agreements / your service base |
| private equity acquisition | the roll-up / the acquisition guys buying you out |
| brand / business reputation | the name on your truck / your name in this town |
| succession plan | who takes it over |

## How they refer to the business & themselves (identity)
the shop • built it from one truck • started out of my garage / my pickup • the trucks / the fleet • **the name on the truck** • my crew / my guys / my techs • I still run calls / I'm still in the field • owner-operator • 30 years serving this town.

## How they talk about the work (use sparingly, for credibility)
service calls • install jobs • the call board / dispatch • busy season / the season / shoulder season • callbacks • **maintenance agreements / service agreements / the maintenance base** • the route / book of business • on-call / after-hours / the pager • journeyman / master plumber / licensed • the warehouse / parts / inventory • "the trucks and the techs."

## How they talk about the worry (the jobs, in their words)
- "Who's going to take care of my regulars?"
- "What happens to my guys — will the new owner keep my crew?"
- "Will they keep the name?"
- "I don't want a roll-up chopping it up / gutting what I built."
- "Is the shop worth anything if I'm not the one running calls?"
- "Can it run without me?"
- "I'm tired of carrying the pager / being on call."
- "My knees / back are shot."
- "The kids don't want to swing wrenches."
- "I've got good techs, but nobody who can run the place."
- "Those acquisition guys keep calling."

## Trigger phrases (great ad hooks & page titles)
- "A roll-up offered to buy my shop — now what?"
- "Buddy down the road just sold his shop."
- "PE keeps calling."
- "I'm 60 and my knees are done."
- "Lost my right-hand guy."

## The values to protect (for the protection-first framing)
the name on the truck • doing right by my customers • taking care of my crew • standing behind the work / my word • the town and community I've served.

## Two phrases that prove you know the trade
- **The maintenance/service-agreement base** — the most valuable, sale-defining asset a shop owns and the first thing a buyer probes. Name it.
- **"Can it run without you when you're off the truck?"** — the real owner-dependency issue, said the way an owner says it.

## Assembled hooks
- "You built it from one truck. Don't hand it to someone who'll gut it."
- "The name on your truck is worth protecting. So is your crew."
- "A roll-up's been calling. Before you call back, know what you'd be giving up."
- "Can your shop run without you on the truck? A buyer's going to ask — better you know first."
- "Your maintenance base is the most valuable thing you own — and the first thing a buyer will dig into."
